# Flow 3 — Presenter Notes: Federation Agreement
**Audience**: Cooperative members, organizers, community leaders (non-technical)
**Duration**: ~15 minutes with pauses
**Key message**: Three independent cooperatives formed an agreement — directly, as equals — without surrendering control to any platform or intermediary.

---

## Beat: Step 0 — Connecting to three nodes
**Say**: "Three cooperatives, three independent nodes. River City Tool Library. BrightWorks. Finger Lakes Community Development Network. None of them is in charge."
**Point to**: The connection output

---

## Beat: Authenticating all three
**Say**: "Each coop authenticates independently. No shared account, no federation administrator."
**Point to**: The auth output

---

## Beat: Step 1 — The situation
**Say**: "River City has a metalworking shop with equipment that sits idle on weekends and evenings. BrightWorks does small-batch fabrication and needs occasional access. They could just make a handshake deal — or they could record it on the network where it's enforceable and verifiable."
**Point to**: The narration
**If asked**: "Yes, the handshake deal works until someone disputes it. The on-network agreement works when the people who made the handshake aren't around anymore."

---

## Beat: Step 2 — Three independent views
**Say**: "Three nodes, three independent cooperative networks. None of them controls the others. When they form a federation agreement, it's voluntary — either party can exit."
**Point to**: The status output

---

## Beat: Step 3 — River City's internal governance
**Say**: "River City governs itself. Their internal decisions — who can use the shop, what the rates are, how members vote — none of that requires approval from BrightWorks or Finger Lakes."
**Point to**: The governance output

---

## Beat: Step 4 — BrightWorks' internal governance
**Say**: "Same for BrightWorks. Fully sovereign. The federation agreement is about what happens at the boundary between them — not about who controls their internal operations."
**Point to**: The governance output

---

## Beat: Step 5 — Finger Lakes as facilitator
**Say**: "Finger Lakes is a CDN — a Community Development Network. Their role here is facilitation. They're not a landlord, not a franchisor. They help enforce the terms that River City and BrightWorks agreed to."
**Point to**: The facilitator reference
**If asked "Why do they need a facilitator?"**: "They don't have to use one. But having a trusted third party vouch for the agreement makes it more credible to outsiders — lenders, funders, other potential partners."

---

## Beat: Step 6 — Federation vouching
**Say**: "This is the core idea of federation: trust without ownership. Finger Lakes is attesting that they've reviewed this agreement and vouch for it. River City and BrightWorks remain fully independent."
**Point to**: The vouching record
**If asked "What does vouching actually do?"**: "It's a reputation signal. Other cooperatives considering working with River City or BrightWorks can see that Finger Lakes — a known, trusted entity — has reviewed and stood behind this agreement."

---

## Beat: Step 7 — Clearing agreement
**Say**: "Here's the agreement: off-peak equipment access from River City in exchange for maintenance hours from BrightWorks. Every exchange gets recorded here — not in a spreadsheet one person controls."
**Point to**: The agreement terms

---

## Beat: Step 8 (if present)
**Say**: "The agreement is live on all three nodes."
**Point to**: The status

---

## Beat: Step 9 — Three nodes, one agreement
**Say**: "This is the moment. Three independent nodes. Each sees the same agreement from their own perspective. If River City's node goes down, the agreement still exists on BrightWorks' and Finger Lakes' nodes."
**Point to**: The three-node view
**If asked**: "Right — there's no central server that could go down and take the agreement with it."

---

## Beat: Step 10 — What federation looks like
**Say**: "This is the whole pitch for federation without centralization. Two cooperatives coordinating — as equals — without a platform in the middle extracting rent and holding their data hostage."
**Point to**: The final state
**If asked "How is this different from just emailing a contract?"**: "An emailed contract is a PDF someone could alter, stored on servers you don't control, enforceable only through legal process. This agreement is held on the coops' own nodes, automatically enforceable by the software, and verifiable by any party the coops grant access to."

---

## Common Questions

**"What if one coop wants to exit the agreement?"**
"They submit an exit proposal. If the terms allow unilateral exit, they can leave. If not, it goes back to negotiation. The terms are part of the agreement itself."

**"What stops a big coop from bullying a small one?"**
"The terms they agreed to. If the agreement says 50/50, both parties have a cryptographic record of that. The small coop can prove the terms in any dispute."

**"Can federations have federations?"**
"Yes. That's the ICN roadmap. Local coops → regional networks → national federations. Each level is voluntary, each is sovereign."
