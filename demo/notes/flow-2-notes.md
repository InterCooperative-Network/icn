# Flow 2 — Presenter Notes: BrightWorks Patronage Distribution
**Audience**: Cooperative members, organizers, community leaders (non-technical)
**Duration**: ~15 minutes with pauses
**Key message**: Surplus went from Q1 close → democratic vote → ledger settlement → member balances. The whole chain is verifiable.

---

## Beat: Step 0 — Connection
**Say**: "BrightWorks is a worker cooperative. They design and build things together. At the end of Q1, they had a surplus to distribute."
**Point to**: The connection output

---

## Beat: Authenticating
**Say**: "Same as before — BrightWorks authenticates with their cooperative identity."
**Point to**: The authentication output

---

## Beat: Step 1 — Q1 has closed
**Say**: "Q1 ended with a surplus. In a worker cooperative, surplus goes back to the workers — not to shareholders. The formula for how to split it is called patronage. Who gets what, based on contribution."
**Point to**: The narration
**If asked "What is patronage?"**: "Patronage allocation is the cooperative equivalent of a dividend — but it goes to member-workers proportional to their contribution (hours worked, in this case), not to investors proportional to their capital."

---

## Beat: Step 2 — Locating the proposal
**Say**: "Before the surplus is distributed, members vote on whether the formula is correct. It's not just the board deciding — the workers themselves confirm the math."
**Point to**: The proposal reference

---

## Beat: Step 3 — The allocation formula
**Say**: "Here's the formula. 120 hours worked → 880 credits. 115 hours → 843 credits. Every member can see this and verify the arithmetic. This isn't a number that came from accounting — it's a publicly visible formula that anyone can check."
**Point to**: The formula in the output
**If asked "What are credits?"**: "Patronage credits are the cooperative's internal accounting of what each member is owed. They can be converted to cash distribution at year end, or retained as member capital — the coop decides."

---

## Beat: Step 4 — Opening the vote
**Say**: "The formula is submitted as a governance proposal. The membership votes to ratify it — or they could reject it and send it back."
**Point to**: The open status

---

## Beat: Step 5 — Members vote
**Say**: "This is the democratic moment. The workers are voting on whether their own allocation formula is correct. They're not just approving it — they're taking responsibility for it."
**Point to**: The votes

---

## Beat: Step 6 — Tally confirmed
**Say**: "Pulling the tally now — if the vote is ratified, the ledger settlement is authorized."
**Point to**: The tally

---

## Beat: Step 7 — Proposal closed
**Say**: "Closed and locked. The next step is execution — moving the credits."
**Point to**: The closed status

---

## Beat: Step 8 — Ledger settlement
**Say**: "880 patronage credits to Yusuf Okafor. The governance decision we just saw authorized this transaction. The ledger records it with a reference back to the proposal that approved it."
**Point to**: The settlement result
**If asked "What happens to the credits?"**: "Members hold them in their cooperative account. Year end, the coop decides whether to convert to cash, retain as member capital, or some combination."

---

## Beat: Step 9 — Live balance
**Say**: "The cooperative account balance is live. Any member can query their own balance at any time — the coop doesn't have to send a statement."
**Point to**: The balance result
**If asked**: "Yes, this is real-time. No waiting for month-end accounting."

---

## Beat: Step 10 — Full history
**Say**: "Here's the whole chain: Q1 surplus → allocation formula → member vote → ledger entry → member balance. Every step traceable. This is what a cooperative financial record looks like when it's on the network."
**Point to**: The history entries

---

## Beat: Step 11 — Receipt chain
**Say**: "This is the foundation for external accountability. A funder, a union, a regulator — anyone BrightWorks chooses to give access to can follow this chain and verify: the coop governed democratically, the formula was member-approved, the distribution happened as stated."
**Point to**: The receipt chain
**If asked**: "Right — this is what makes grant reporting real. Not a PDF anyone could have written — a verifiable chain of decisions and transactions."

---

## Common Questions

**"What if a member disputes the allocation?"**
"They raise a new governance proposal. The dispute itself becomes part of the democratic record."

**"Can the coop change the formula after the fact?"**
"They'd have to pass a new proposal. The old allocation is already settled — they can only adjust going forward."

**"What's the difference between this and just using QuickBooks?"**
"QuickBooks is owned by Intuit, held on their servers, and visible only to the people with access. This record is held across the cooperative's own nodes, verifiable by any member with authorization, and not dependent on any vendor."
