A substrate for what survives after formation.
Standing, authority, decisions, allocations, and the layer of cooperative memory that has to survive every handoff that follows.
What survives the platform is not the platform. It is the standing, the authority, the decision, the allocation, and the receipt that an outside party can verify years later — a future board, a CPA, an auditor, a member who joined after the founders rotated out.

Cooperative-formation platforms make the work of starting a co-op doable — steps, documents, teams, advisors, milestones. ICN is asking the next layer of question. Once those steps become real institutional facts, how does the cooperative remember them — across founder rotations, advisor turnover, fiscal years, and the inevitable platform migration? Where do internal capital account records, patronage records, governance decisions, and member standing survive as durable evidence, not as another fragile spreadsheet?

What ICN is

ICN is a substrate daemon for cooperative coordination. Not a SaaS app, not a blockchain, not a payment system, and not a replacement for the formation work that any platform or advisor is already doing. It is a constraint enforcement engine that a cooperative — or a federation of cooperatives — runs on its own infrastructure, with records signed in a way an outside party can verify later without trusting us.

The architecture has a deliberate firewall. Cooperative meaning — trust, governance rules, membership criteria, patronage allocation logic — lives in apps that the cooperative governs. The kernel enforces the resulting constraints blindly. This keeps the substrate predictable while leaving the meaning under democratic control.

Where ICN actually has substrate

  • Cooperative formation lifecycle in code — Forming → CharterSigned → CharterRatified → Activated → Suspended/Resumed → DissolutionStarted → AssetsDistributed → Dissolved. Treasury DID derived deterministically per coop. Founder signatures Ed25519-signed with role and timestamp.
  • Charter as enforced policy, not a PDF — YAML charter documents become a policy oracle that emits kernel-enforced constraints. The coop's bylaws gate every future decision the substrate evaluates.
  • Mutual credit ledger with double-entry journaling, append-only, signed entries, vector-clock-ordered. A state-change journal an outside party can verify years later.
  • Receipt envelopes for every state transition. Every governance decision, every allocation, every standing change leaves a verifiable artifact.
  • Labor exchange + credit routing across cooperatives — assignment types (project / seasonal / trial / dual membership), routing modes (through home coop / direct / split), governance approvals from both home and host coops required.
  • Federation primitives — gossip-discovered cooperative registry, federated trust attestations, bilateral clearing agreements, multilateral debt-cycle netting, scoped gossip, federated DID resolution.
  • Vocabulary discipline — settlement, obligation, allocation, position, receipt, provenance, evidence. The substrate avoids financial-product semantics by design.

What ICN is not

These are commitments, not modesty.

  • Not accounting software.
  • Not a payment app or wallet.
  • Not a money transmitter.
  • Not a bank or financial intermediary.
  • Not a replacement for any formation platform.
  • Not a replacement for lawyers, CPAs, bookkeepers, or co-op developers.
  • Not a blockchain or token system.
  • Not production-ready.
  • Not externally audited.
  • Not a formal pilot.

A receipt is not a legitimacy claim. Receipts prove that an event happened with the signatures attached; legitimacy comes from the cooperative's own process — who held standing, who held authority, what the governance document said. The receipt is just the part that survives.

What this conversation is for

Find one cooperative project — yours or one you have watched closely — where the patronage record, the internal capital account, the membership roster, or the governance trail did not survive what it needed to survive. Strip the names and the numbers. Bring the shape of the failure. We walk it through the spine together; that walk is the rehearsal.

Three outcomes are useful: a sanitized scenario worth working through, a clear "not yet" with reasons, or a handoff to whoever should be in the next room — a CPA, a cooperative attorney, a federation organizer, a TA provider.
