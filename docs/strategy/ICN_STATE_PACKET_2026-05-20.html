Intercooperative Network Snapshot · 2026-05-20
ICN · where it stands.
Substrate for cooperative coordination. Research stage, single maintainer, running on a small K3s cluster since December 2025. Not a SaaS app, not a blockchain, not production.

One-paragraph project frame

ICN (Intercooperative Network) is a substrate daemon for the cooperative internet — a constraint enforcement engine that cooperatives, communities, and federations run on their own infrastructure to coordinate without central servers. The core architectural commitment is the meaning firewall: apps (governance, ledger, membership) translate domain meaning into generic constraints, and the kernel enforces those constraints without understanding what they mean. This keeps the substrate predictable while leaving cooperative meaning under democratic control.

The meaning firewall

The architectural commitment that makes everything else legible: kernel code never imports domain types. Apps translate domain meaning (trust scores, governance rules, membership criteria, patronage rules) into a generic ConstraintSet. The kernel sees only constraints and decisions, never the semantic origin. A trust-policy oracle, for example, computes a trust score and converts it into a rate limit + credit multiplier; the kernel enforces those limits without knowing they came from trust.

This means the kernel stays small and predictable, and cooperative governance can adapt the meaning layer without rewriting infrastructure. It also means apps are policy oracles — they answer "is this allowed, and with what shape?" without giving the kernel any reason to care why.

See: docs/architecture/KERNEL_APP_SEPARATION.md

The receipt chain

Every state transition produces a receipt envelope (ADR-0026). Receipts are signed, opaque to non-authorized readers (storage cascade), and form an audit trail. A receipt is not a legitimacy claim — it proves the event happened with the signatures attached. Legitimacy comes from the cooperative's process: who held standing, who held authority, what the governance document said. This separation is doctrinal and is documented explicitly in the abuse-case hardening strategy.

See: docs/architecture/ABUSE_CASE_HARDENING_STRATEGY.md · ADR-0026

Vocabulary discipline

Avoided: currency, token, exchange, transfer, wallet, send, payment, crypto. Used instead: settlement, obligation, allocation, position, receipt, provenance, evidence, unit. The choice is to keep ICN-native primitives from claiming financial-product semantics. Whether the resulting posture is sufficient for any specific jurisdiction is an attorney question, not an architecture one.

What it would take to scale further

Resource needs, framed against what's already built — not asking from zero.

PeopleSingle maintainer today. Broadening to two or three would let federation, mobile, and steward-cockpit work proceed in parallel rather than serial. Closest near-term needs: a Rust contributor for the federation-clearing path and a front-end engineer for the steward + member surfaces.
ComputeThree-node K3s handles the pilot. Beyond ~10-coop federation rehearsal, the cluster needs more workers — either additional Proxmox hosts or capacity on dedicated coop infrastructure as partners come online.
StorageAtlas NFS is the single backing store. Adequate for receipt history at pilot scale; needs an off-host replica plan before any real-coop pilot stores production data.
Bandwidth + edgeOPNsense edge handles current QUIC traffic. Multi-federation rehearsal across geographically distinct nodes is the next stress test.
AuditExternal security review — substrate + cryptography + key handling — before any production-coop pilot.
Legal counselCooperative attorney + regulatory attorney engagement before any specific deployment claims about jurisdictional fit.
Funded organizer timeFor the institution-package work (Summit, formation, federation) — practitioners who shape the meaning layer should be paid for it, not volunteering on the side of full-time co-op work. This is what Summit organizers themselves identified as the blocker (Lessons Learned, Dec 2025).

The five things that can't move

  • Public by default ≠ private info made public. It means everything affecting members is auditable through the channels those members consented to.
  • Treasury visible to members. Legitimacy of process is auditable through channels affected parties consent to.
  • Receipts prove events, not legitimacy. Legitimacy lives in the cooperative's process.
  • Meaning in apps; kernel predictable. Constraint engine, not policy engine.
  • Regulatory vocabulary discipline. Settlement, obligation, allocation, position, receipt, provenance, evidence. Never currency, token, transfer, wallet.
  • Anti-software boundaries. Some things must stay human and process-first. Some records should expire. Some should never be public.
