A substrate for what survives after formation.
Standing, authority, decisions, allocations, and the layer of cooperative memory that has to survive every handoff after formation.

Launch makes cooperative formation doable. ICN is asking the next layer of question: once those steps become real institutional facts, how does the co-op remember them — across founder rotations, advisor handoffs, fiscal years, and the inevitable platform migration?

What ICN is

ICN is a substrate daemon for cooperative coordination. It's not a SaaS app, not a blockchain, not a payment system, and not a replacement for the work Launch is doing. It's a constraint enforcement engine that a cooperative (or federation of cooperatives) runs on its own infrastructure, with records signed in a way an outside party — a future board, a CPA, an auditor, a buyer of a member's interest — can verify later without trusting us.

The architecture has a deliberate firewall: cooperative meaning (trust, governance rules, membership criteria, patronage allocation logic) lives in apps that the cooperative governs. The kernel enforces constraints blindly. This keeps the substrate predictable while leaving the meaning under democratic control.

Where ICN actually has substrate

  • Mutual credit ledger with double-entry journaling, append-only, signed entries, vector-clock-ordered. The state-change journal a co-op's CPA or board could verify years later.
  • Receipt envelopes for every state transition (ADR-0026). Every governance decision, every allocation, every standing change leaves a verifiable artifact.
  • Vocabulary discipline — settlement, obligation, allocation, position, receipt, provenance, evidence. The substrate avoids financial-product semantics by design.

What ICN is not

These are commitments, not modesty.

  • Not accounting software.
  • Not a payment app or wallet.
  • Not a money transmitter.
  • Not a bank or financial intermediary.
  • Not a Launch replacement.
  • Not a replacement for lawyers, CPAs, bookkeepers, or co-op developers.
  • Not a blockchain or token system.
  • Not production-ready.
  • Not externally audited.
  • Not a formal pilot.

A receipt is not a legitimacy claim. Receipts prove that an event happened with the signatures attached; legitimacy comes from the cooperative's own process — who had standing, who held authority, what the governance document said. The receipt is just the part that survives.

What this conversation is for

A reciprocal conversation. I want to learn more about Launch's workflow and ecosystem, share enough of the ICN shape to make the question intelligible, and find out where the map is wrong. Specific interest, from your May 1 email: patronage and capital-account history as institutional memory that survives founder rotation and advisor handoffs.

The most useful outcome would be one of three things:

  • A sanitized rehearsal scenario — a fictional five-member worker co-op, one allocation cycle, one disputed entry — that we walk through together to test whether the seam holds.
  • A "not yet" with specifics about what would need to be true for the seam to be worth working.
  • A handoff to others — a CPA, co-op lawyer, federation organizer, or co-op developer whose perspective should be in the room before the conversation goes further.
